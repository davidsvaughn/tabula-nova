## Are we onto something? Plain-English answer

**A small, inconclusive positive hint—not a demonstrated breakthrough. LimiX has
the best later CPI score, but improves on ordinary learned methods by only about
2%, and the statistical check cannot establish that this advantage is real.**

- **Negative evidence:** in the completed GDP-contraction experiment, the
  economists' survey forecast beat every tested TabPFN configuration and LimiX.
  The learned adjustments made forecasts worse overall. The earlier volatility
  pilot also favored ordinary ridge regression over TabPFN.
- **Small positive hint:** in the 2020–2026 CPI evaluation, ordinary ridge and
  boosted trees improved the main forecast-error score by about 12% versus simply
  predicting the historical average. Their average point error fell from about
  **0.274 to 0.239 percentage points**. That is useful evidence of some signal,
  but the historical average is a weak opponent. The foundation models must beat
  these stronger ordinary methods, not merely beat the average.
  **This is not an across-period finding:** in 2010–2019 the mean beat both
  ordinary learned models. LOG-010's post-result breakdown shows most of their
  later gain over the mean was concentrated in 2020–2022.
- **Warning:** the ordinary models' supposedly 80% intervals contained the actual
  CPI result only 69–73% of the time. They underestimated uncertainty during this
  period. Better average point predictions alone would not solve that problem.
- **A little more support:** ridge's roughly 11% improvement over the historical
  average narrowly passes the declared statistical uncertainty check. Boosted
  trees' similar improvement does not. This strengthens the case for some CPI
  signal, not for a major advantage or foundation-model skill.
- **TabPFN now tested:** it beats the historical average, but is slightly worse
  than ridge and boosted trees in the later period. The uncertainty ranges do
  not establish a difference from those stronger methods. No added skill is
  demonstrated; a small advantage in earlier years did not persist.
- **LimiX now tested:** its later distribution score is best, about 2.2% better
  than boosted trees. Average point error falls from ridge's 0.239 to 0.233
  percentage points—only 0.006 percentage points better. This is a weak positive
  hint worth a carefully controlled follow-on, not evidence of a major edge.
  Its uncertainty range includes no improvement over ridge/trees, its 80%
  intervals cover only 70.7%, and its `>0.3%` probabilities do not beat ridge.

**What would be genuinely encouraging:** lower forecast error than the strongest
ordinary model in the later, frozen evaluation period, with the predeclared
uncertainty check supporting the difference and reasonably honest prediction
intervals. Even that would be evidence for this limited CPI experiment—not proof
of beating professional nowcasts, markets, broad superforecasting or making money.

**Why continue:** LimiX's small lead makes a limited, independently evaluated
richer-input experiment reasonable. CPI history alone cannot supply timely news
about every energy/supply shock. Better inputs might help, but that is a separate
hypothesis—not a result already in hand. Do not chase settings on these outcomes.

## Prediction hypotheses, not assumed capabilities

1. **Nonlinear small-sample signal.** With identical original-print lags 1/2/3/12 and sine/cosine seasonality, TabPFN-3.5 or LimiX-2 may predict CPI better than historical mean, standardized ridge or histogram boosting. Primary comparison is confirmation-period CRPS against the strongest declared conventional baseline, not merely against the weakest mean model. Lower MAE alone does not establish better probability forecasts. A negative finding is useful: preserve it rather than add features or tune on confirmation.
2. **Distributional usefulness.** A model's point forecasts plus the same declared past-only residual-distribution procedure may improve CRPS, 10/50/90 quantile losses and derived strict `>0.3%` Brier, while maintaining defensible 80% interval coverage. We are testing predictive distributions, not unrelated classifiers per strike. The shared empirical-residual procedure is deliberately a controlled uncertainty method; its result is not a comparison of every model's native posterior or a causal claim.
3. **Regime robustness.** Any gain should survive the frozen 2020-01–2026-08 confirmation interval, not only the 2010–2019 development interval. Report both periods, individual monthly predictions and excluded months. Post-pandemic/shutdown behavior is not discarded to rescue a favorable aggregate. A 12-month block bootstrap quantifies paired uncertainty; overlapping threshold contracts never count as independent evidence.
4. **Later forecast combination, not this initial run.** Cleveland nowcasts or rule-matched market probabilities may add timely information or benefit from learned corrections. This is a distinct future configuration requiring defensible historical forecast/quote clocks. It is not monthly survey consensus, and cannot be inferred from a lag-only model win. No such combination score is authorized by merely having downloaded a dated history.

The first experiment tests hypotheses 1–3. Hypothesis 4 remains separately gated. These are hypotheses about this target, information set and frozen configurations, not a general verdict on macro forecasting, foundation-model architecture, superforecasting or trading profitability.
