# LOG-010 — Step back: forecast reliability, not another model rematch

Date: 2026-10-01. User asked to pause, review all project documents and both original chats, and decide what is most interesting/promising next. **No new model forecasts, configurations, collectors or trading actions were run during this review.**

## Recommendation in plain English

**Study when a good existing economic forecast is likely to be wrong, and by how much.** Use TabPFN/LimiX as possible forecast correctors and uncertainty estimators—not assume that they should replace professional forecasts.

The best first case is **energy-informed CPI forecasting around a real statistical nowcast**, with simple corrections as serious competitors. This is not a recommendation to keep tuning until LimiX's small lead becomes convincing. It is a different, more informative question:

> Does timely economic evidence help us predict the mistakes or uncertainty of an existing inflation forecast—and do the foundation models extract anything that ordinary methods miss?

Conditional forecast combination remains the longer-term direction. Start with the information we can actually defend, not an imagined table of market, economist and LLM forecasts. Monthly economist consensus has not been acquired; Cleveland is a statistical nowcast, not that consensus. Kalshi is not required for this first scientific test.

**Confidence:** moderate that this is the most informative next study; low that either foundation model will beat the strong alternatives. A useful result can be an improved ordinary forecast or a well-supported decision to retain the existing nowcast.

## New descriptive diagnosis: regime and measurement resolution

A throwaway CPU-only Python analysis inspected the **existing** five CPI result artifacts. It made no new predictions and selected no model settings. The following subdivisions are **post-result descriptive analysis**, not new confirmation:

| Period | Events | Mean CRPS | Ridge | Boosted trees | TabPFN | LimiX |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2010–2019 |120|0.138851|0.139811|0.146351|0.134529|0.139192|
| 2020–2022 |36|0.267392|0.223461|0.218248|0.231932|0.220201|
| 2023–2026 Aug |39|0.144317|0.140152|0.144326|0.137564|0.135031|

The mean was the best **ordinary** baseline in 2010–2019. The large learned-baseline improvement over the mean is concentrated in 2020–2022; boosted trees were then slightly better than LimiX. LimiX's later aggregate lead is not an across-regime domination. The cause of those differences is untested.

To understand why a small lead is inconclusive, the diagnostic reused `scripts/cpi_compare.py:block_indices`: 2,000 draws of 12-calendar-month blocks, seed 17, the full 80-month later calendar with five gaps. Bootstrap standard deviations of paired mean CRPS differences were:

- LimiX minus ridge: **0.004755**.
- LimiX minus boosted trees: **0.005833**.

Under a **conditional normal approximation**, a two-sided 5% test with 80% power would need an effect approximately `(1.96 + 0.842) × SE`: **0.01332 / 0.01634 CRPS**, about **7.4% / 9.1%** of these baseline scores. This is a retrospective noise-scale diagnostic, **not** certified prospective power, an exact minimum detectable effect, or an estimate valid for a changed information set. Few long time blocks, nonstationarity, bootstrap bias and multiple comparisons limit it.

Decision: a single monthly family is poorly positioned to establish a roughly 2% model advantage quickly. Before another run, choose the practical effect worth detecting and evaluate whether the eligible events can resolve it. Do not promise that a few future releases will settle the issue.

## Why this direction, and why not the alternatives first?

1. **Lead: conditional correction/uncertainty around CPI nowcasts.** Near-release inflation has a plausible information source missing from our lag study: observed retail energy prices. Unlike an unforeseen next-quarter GDP shock, some of the target month's price movement has already occurred. Whether this helps beyond Cleveland is untested; Cleveland may already use overlapping information. The real question is incremental value, not adding correlated sources and declaring improvement.
2. **Backup: continuous SPF forecast-error/uncertainty correction.** GDP growth magnitudes retain information that a rare contraction label discards. Survey disagreement and trajectory may predict error scale. But roughly 139 possible modern quarterly origins, already-studied macro regimes and unpredictable GDP shocks do not offer an obvious power advantage. Individual respondents are not independent GDP outcomes. Summaries of one survey alone are **consensus correction**, not multiple-source combination.
3. **Complement later: a small prospective broad economic-question portfolio.** Own timestamped forecasts can address policy, labor, energy and technology questions without Metaculus archive dependence. Valuable for breadth and learning, but rare/long-horizon outcomes cannot quickly validate a foundation-model combiner. No orchestration/dependency-graph build is justified yet.

Do not prioritize another configuration sweep, raw stock-price prediction, immediate heterogeneous-country pooling, an unaudited market archive, or a Preseen-style scenario platform. Weather/event families could eventually offer more observations, but our documents do not establish a ready, permitted, clock-safe replacement benchmark. This review found no evidence-backed shortcut to a general superforecaster.

The SPF reviewer initially favored continuous SPF based on documentation/access, then revised that ranking after challenge: source convenience does not demonstrate more learnable residuals or greater statistical power. That disagreement helped separate the scientific question from operational convenience.

## Fresh primary-source checks that affect feasibility

The EIA [Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/) exposes weekly national prices and links full-history Excel files without an API-key interaction. **No energy history was downloaded or integrated in this review.**

The currently published [release schedule](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/schedule.php) and [gasoline methodology](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/gas_proc-methods.php), read on 2026-10-01, say:

- Measurements represent **8am local Monday** cash pump prices, including taxes.
- Publication is approximately **10am Eastern Tuesday**, or Wednesday under stated government-holiday exceptions—not an assumed Monday 5pm release.
- A sample/method change on **2018-05-14** makes that estimate not directly comparable to the previous week's old-sample estimate.

Thus the measurement Monday cannot be used as the publication clock. Current schedule text does not certify every historical week's posting time; historical schedules, errors/revisions and terms still require a bounded audit. A current Excel download does not prove its original bytes were available at a past forecast cutoff.

The opportunity review also checked official [SPF individual forecasts](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/individual-forecasts), [FAQ](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/spf-faqs) and [documentation](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/FRBP/Assets/Surveys-And-Data/survey-of-professional-forecasters/spf-documentation.pdf?sc_lang=en&hash=8408A4F1BF351A3C268B40F6BC7B95AA). Important backup-design constraints:

- The current individual point/density archive exists, but its full continuous-target workbook has not been shape-audited here. Historical public microdata posting is not certified by a survey-results date.
- Published aggregate GDP growth is growth of **aggregate forecast levels**, not the mean of individually calculated growth rates. Preserve the official benchmark rather than silently substituting the latter.
- Annual density respondents/bins/horizons do not create independent annual outcomes; their small annual sample and changing bins make them a weak shortcut to a large probability-learning benchmark.

## The discriminating experiment to design—not launch during this pause

Keep the original CPI target/rounding contract. Use matched cutoff information and separate:

1. **Information value:** lag/seasonal inputs versus the same inputs plus one carefully defined retail-gasoline measure, for ordinary methods as well as the native models.
2. **Correction value:** raw Cleveland nowcast plus a past-only error distribution versus simple bias/conditional-scale corrections and a small evidence-conditioned correction. Cleveland itself must remain a benchmark.
3. **Model value:** only then ask whether unchanged TabPFN/LimiX add a meaningful proper-score improvement beyond the strongest simple correction on the same events.

Prespecify uncertainty methods. Include a simple conditional uncertainty competitor rather than repeating only an unconditional trailing-error wrapper. Test documented TabPFN native quantiles/distribution as a bounded secondary method; do **not** assume LimiX exposes an equivalent usable native distribution without checking its actual output semantics. Point forecast disagreement is not automatically outcome uncertainty.

Score the full distribution, interval coverage/width and the declared threshold probabilities; point error is secondary. No market-return claim. Use historical work as explicitly exploratory where vintages/previously seen periods require that qualification; actual prospective confirmation needs forecasts frozen before outcomes. Both weight licenses must remain compatible with noncommercial research.
